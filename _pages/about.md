<section class="section section--editorial">

      <span class="eyebrow eyebrow--muted">About</span>
<h2>Hello! Hola! <a href="https://ml.wikipedia.org/wiki/%E0%B4%A8%E0%B4%AE%E0%B4%B8%E0%B5%8D%E0%B4%95%E0%B4%BE%E0%B4%B0%E0%B4%82" lang="ml">നമസ്കാരം</a>!</h2>

    </div>

    <div class="prose">
      <p>
        I am a postdoc at
        <a href="https://www.as.arizona.edu/">Steward Observatory</a>, University of
        Arizona, working with Prof. David J. Sand.
        My research follows core-collapse supernovae from their first hours to
        thousands of days after explosion: I use the
        <a href="https://jwst.nasa.gov/">James Webb Space Telescope</a> to watch cold
        dust condense in supernova ejecta, chase the earliest light from very young
        nearby transients, and search for the electromagnetic counterparts of
        gravitational-wave events with Arizona's telescopes.
      </p>

      <p>
        I completed my Ph.D. in Physics and Astronomy at
        <a href="https://www.purdue.edu/">Purdue University</a> with
        Dr. <a href="https://www.physics.purdue.edu/milisavljevic/people.html">Danny Milisavljevic</a>,
        where I built statistical inference machinery to pull explosion parameters out of
        sparse all-sky survey light curves, and helped develop
        <a href="https://refitt.physics.purdue.edu/">REFITT</a>, an AI system that
        recommends which transients to follow up, and when. Along the way I led the
        discovery paper on
        <a href="{{ '/research/#at2021lwx' | relative_url }}">AT&nbsp;2021lwx</a>, better
        known as &ldquo;Scary Barbie,&rdquo; one of the most energetic non-quasar optical transients ever
        recorded, now belonging to the class called Extreme Nuclear Transients (ENTs).
      </p>

      <p>
        Beyond my academic work, I’m passionate about programming, data science , and the application of machine learning in astronomy. I’m also a strong advocate for women and underrepresented groups in STEM , and I take pride in participating in leadership roles that promote diversity and inclusion in science. I spent three
        years with Purdue's
        <a href="https://www.purdue.edu/science/wisp/graduate/index.html">Women in Science Program</a>
        and served as president of the Physics Graduate Student Association, and I now
        mentor undergraduate and graduate students on projects of their own. <br> <br>
Feel free to explore my work or reach out if you’re interested in collaborating!
      </p>
    </div>
  </div>
</section>

        Search for the elusive host galaxy of the extreme nuclear transient AT 2021lwx, using deep HST and JWST imaging to uncover its faint environment and shed light on the nature and origin of this extraordinary transient.

      <a class="card card--link" href="{{ '/research/#jwst-dust' | relative_url }}">
        <span class="card__meta">JWST &middot; MIRI</span>
        <h3 class="card__title">Dust in core-collapse supernovae</h3>
        <p>
          A JWST/MIRI sample of eleven normal Type&nbsp;IIP supernovae spanning
          400&ndash;2300 days, asking when and how much dust ordinary explosions make. Can normal CCSNe produce dust to account for the dust we see in the early Unierse? 
        </p>
        <span class="card__foot">Read more &rarr;</span>
      </a>

      <a class="card card--link" href="{{ '/research/#young-transients' | relative_url }}">
        <span class="card__meta">Shock cooling &middot; Progenitors</span>
        <h3 class="card__title"> Early Observations of Supernovae</h3>
        <p>
          Early observations of very young supernovae like the Type IIb
          SN 2024uwq that constrain can be used to constrain the progenitor star that exploded. The bigger question is to answer what stars explode as what type of supernovae? How can early observations of the explosion help us understand these systems better?
        </p>
        <span class="card__foot">Read more &rarr;</span>
      </a>

      <a class="card card--link" href="{{ '/research/#gravitational-waves' | relative_url }}">
        <span class="card__meta">SAGUARO &middot; TROVE</span>
        <h3 class="card__title">Gravitational-wave follow-up in search of electromagnetic (EM) counterparts </h3>
        <p>
          Leading MMT and Magellan follow-up of LIGO&ndash;Virgo&ndash;KAGRA (LVK) GW alerts with
          SAGUARO, and building the vetting tools that tell a kilonova apart from the
          supernovae crowding the same sky.
        </p>
        <span class="card__foot">Read more &rarr;</span>
      </a>

        <span class="card__meta">LSST &middot; Follow-up</span>

        <p>
        Developing science-driven strategies to identify, prioritize, and follow up transients discovered across wide-field sky surveys, enabling rapid characterization of the most interesting and anomalous events.
